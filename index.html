Three places EIRs work

The title means different things depending on who hires you. EIRcamp prepares you for all three.

At a startup

An "internal founder". You work with the founder to launch a new product, vertical or market from scratch, own its P&L, and often hand it over once it runs without you.

Often listed as
EIR, founder's office, new initiatives

At a VC fund

Usually an experienced operator or founder. You help the fund review startups and support its portfolio, while working out your own next company.

Often ends in
A new company the fund backs, or a leadership role in a portfolio company

Through an incubator

A fellowship to work on your own idea full-time. The government's NIDHI-EIR scheme, for example, supports aspiring founders with up to ₹30,000 a month through host incubators.

Often ends in
Your own registered startup

Companies that started with a desk at a fund

Some well-known founders spent time inside a venture firm before, or while, starting their company.

  • Jeff Hammerbacher and Amr AwadallahEIRs at Accel, 2008

    Hammerbacher had built Facebook's data team. He and Awadallah founded Cloudera in Accel's Palo Alto office while they were both entrepreneurs in residence there. Accel then led Cloudera's first funding round.

    Source: Accel

  • Nir ZukGreylock, 2005

    After co-founding OneSecure and serving as CTO of NetScreen, Zuk started Palo Alto Networks from a desk inside Greylock's office, as both Greylock and Palo Alto Networks tell it. It's now one of the world's largest cybersecurity companies.

    Source: Greylock

  • Jason CalacanisEIR at Sequoia Capital, 2006–07

    After running Netscape at AOL, Calacanis joined Sequoia as an entrepreneur in residence, came up with the idea for the search engine Mahalo there, and launched it with Sequoia as lead investor. He later became one of Uber's earliest angel investors.

    Source: TechCrunch

  • Amit JainEIR at Sequoia Capital India, 2022

    The former head of Uber in Asia-Pacific stepped down as a managing director at Sequoia India to start his own company, and stayed on as an entrepreneur in residence while he planned it.

    Source: Business Today

These founders aren't connected to EIRcamp. They're here because their paths show what the role can lead to.

Questions

What is an entrepreneur in residence?

Someone hired to build something new from zero. At a startup, that's usually a new product or vertical. At a VC fund, it's helping review startups while working on your own next company. At an incubator, it's a fellowship to work on your own idea.

Will EIRcamp get me an EIR job?

We can't promise a job. You'll leave with a portfolio of the work EIR roles involve, practice at EIR interviews, and a list of companies and funds that hire for these roles.

Do I need to have founded a company?

No. Many job posts prefer founder experience, and the bootcamp gives you the closest thing to it: you take one new business from idea to first customers and document every step.

How much experience do I need?

Most EIR job posts we read ask for 1 to 7 years of work experience. Final-year students and MBA candidates are welcome too, if they can give six to eight hours a week.

Can I do this while working full-time?

Yes. Most of the work is self-paced, and the live sessions are on Saturday morning and Wednesday evening.

Can I work on an idea for my current employer?

You can use a company you know as the setting for your work, but check your employer's policies first. Many participants choose a public company or a startup they admire instead.
