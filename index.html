Three places EIRs work

The title means different things depending on who hires you. EIRcamp prepares you for all three.

At a startup or company

An "internal founder". You work with the founder to launch a new product, vertical or market from scratch, own its P&L, and often hand it over once it runs without you.

Often listed as
EIR, founder's office, chief of staff, new initiatives

At a VC fund

Usually an experienced operator or founder. You help the fund review startups and support its portfolio, while working out your own next company.

Often ends in
A new company the fund backs, or a leadership role in a portfolio company

Through an incubator

A fellowship to work on your own idea full-time. The government's NIDHI-EIR scheme, for example, supports aspiring founders with up to ₹30,000 a month through host incubators.

Often ends in
Your own registered startup

Where well-known builders started

EIR-style roles come in many forms: shadowing a CEO, working in a founder's office, or sitting at a fund's desk. Some of the best-known founders and business builders started in exactly these seats.

  • Andy JassyJeff Bezos's "shadow" at Amazon, 2002–03

    Jassy spent about a year as Bezos's first technical adviser, sitting in on all of his meetings, much like a founder's office role. In 2003 he left that job to work out a new business for Amazon. It became AWS, and in 2021 Jassy became Amazon's CEO.

    Source: CNBC

  • Asish MohapatraMatrix Partners India, then OfBusiness

    Mohapatra spent over four years at Matrix Partners India, a venture fund, after IIT Kharagpur and ISB. He then co-founded OfBusiness and its lending arm Oxyzo, and both became unicorns.

    Source: Entrepreneur India

These people aren't connected to EIRcamp. They're here because their paths show where a seat next to a founder or a fund can lead.

Questions

What is an entrepreneur in residence?

Someone hired to build something new from zero. At a startup, that's usually a new product or vertical. At a VC fund, it's helping review startups while working on your own next company. At an incubator, it's a fellowship to work on your own idea.

Will EIRcamp get me an EIR job?

We can't promise a job. You'll leave with a portfolio of the work EIR roles involve, practice at EIR interviews, and a list of companies and funds that hire for these roles.

Do I need to have founded a company?

No. Founder experience helps, and the bootcamp gives you the closest thing to it: you take one new business from idea to first customers and document every step.

How much experience do I need?

EIRcamp is designed for people with 1 to 7 years of work experience. Final-year students and MBA candidates are welcome too, if they can give 26–28 hours a week.

Can I do this while working full-time?

It's demanding. Expect 6–8 hours of coursework and about 20 hours of project work every week. It suits people between roles, students, and people with flexible or part-time work. Live sessions are on Saturday morning and Wednesday evening.

Can I work on an idea for my current employer?

You can use a company you know as the setting for your work, but check your employer's policies first. Many participants choose a public company or a startup they admire instead.

Is the fee refundable?

Generally, no. Once you've paid, the fee isn't refundable, but if something unexpected comes up, write to us and we'll look at your case individually.
